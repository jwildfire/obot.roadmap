The situation, in three sentences

Goal #79's Intent/Boundaries no longer describe what @jwildfire actually wants built, the missing detail is partly tacit and partly not yet known even to him, and A3/A4 of the app-plan artifact are held open until it is captured. Asking him to write it down is the one method known to fail: experts unintentionally omit up to ~70% of what matters when describing from scratch, and a single open interview captures roughly a third of a domain. The question this artifact answers is how to run the extraction — not what the answers are.

What the three research strands found

1 · The "grill-me" skill exists — his memory is accurate

grill-me is a real, widely-installed (~800k installs) Claude Code skill by Matt Pocock. It is a one-line wrapper over a grilling engine whose mechanics are excellent and worth adopting: model the subject as a design tree; ask only the frontier (questions whose prerequisites are settled); every question carries the agent's recommended answer so a round is answerable by number; facts are never the user's job — agents look them up, only decisions reach the human. But it is deliberately stateless and single-session ("~46 questions across four rounds is an ordinary session"), which fails the #79 shape outright. Pocock's own resumable sibling, wayfinder, contributes the missing half: a persistent map of decision tickets, one resolved per session, with an explicit "fog of war" section for questions you can tell are coming but cannot yet phrase — exactly the "he does not have all of it in his head" problem. The term also has local history: obot.agent/interviews/p004-grill-queue.md is literally titled "P004 grill-me queue" (he answered its questions in May 2026), running on the Telegram-era stakeholder-interview skill — which supplied the capture schema (verbatim Answer / operational Decision / Follow-up artifacts) our skill forks.

2 · The CSR precedent — why it worked, and its two holes

The exercise he remembers is the CSR change-request framework report (2026-07-27; his memory likely blends it with the platform gap analysis of 07-25, the only artifact literally named "gap analysis"). It was not an interview. It was a pre-built, source-linked proposal — 18-product landscape, an 11-step as-is workflow table, five increments — ending in five numbered questions each carrying a draft position. His cost: a 3-sentence brief in, a ~30-minute read, one decisive message out. The single most important observation: the only tacit requirement it surfaced (hub#131, study-level GitHub repos) arrived as an unsolicited objection to the agent's sequencing — not as an answer to any question asked. And the contrast case is stark: the decide-by-exception questions were all answered same-day, while the open-ended "pick from these 12 proposals" gate (hub#114 D5) has never been answered, three weeks on. The two holes to fix: every one of its questions was about mechanism and none about boundaries/ownership (both misses landed there), and blanket approval of five draft positions in one clause recorded zero signal about which he actually holds opinions on.

3 · The literature — what survives an LLM interviewer over chat

FindingDesign consequence
Experts omit up to ~70% of critical information in free description (Clark & Feldon, cognitive task analysis); one open interview captures ~27–48% of a domain (Aranda et al., 12 quasi-experiments); structured elicitation has effect size g≈0.87 (Tofel-Grehl & Feldon 2013)Never run a blank-page "what do you want?" interview; plan multiple passes and let a fog-of-war ledger carry the tail
The instrument beats the interviewer: analyst experience shows no effect, structured protocols do (Agarwal & Tanniru; Davis et al. RE'06 systematic review — structured interviews beat card sorts, ranking, think-aloud)Encode the protocol as a skill; a checklist-driven agent is enough, a clever one is not the point
Reaction to ONE concrete design is systematically inflated; criticism unlocks with a comparison set of 3 (Tohidi et al., CHI 2006); critique without a supplied lens is nearly worthless, premortem framing is the strongest lens (Veinott et al. 2010); role-played "devil's advocate" gets discounted (Nemeth 2001)Show three genuinely divergent goal drafts, never one; frame critique passes (premortem, perspective reading); never announce a straw man as bait
LLM interviewers already perform near human level on coverage (LLMREI, RE 2025: 73.7% of planted requirements; mistake-guided question generation preferred over human analysts' 68% of the time) — but chat read-backs manufacture false agreement (81% user confirmation vs 72% coder agreement) and models flip under conversational pushback while judging correctly in evaluative framing; only a hard devil's-advocate ROLE produces real dissent (99.2% vs ~55% for "push back" prompt lines)Separate interviewer / critic roles; quote-anchored read-backs asking "find what's wrong", never "is this right?"; challenges routed through a separate evaluative call
Loading the agent with context suppresses clarifying questions — models resolve ambiguity from priors instead of asking; closed/multiple-choice framing systematically truncates answers (Schuman & Presser)Run the interviewer context-light (the context-rich agent preps and critiques); every menu is preceded by an open turn and carries a live "none of these" escape
No published study exists of an LLM interviewing a single expert repeatedly and asynchronously about a product they ownThis deployment is an experiment, not applied known practice — instrument it (the ledger records everything) and expect to tune the protocol after round one

The recommended method (shipped as /grill-me)

A three-layer hybrid, each layer earning its place from a different strand:

  • CSR-style reactive substrate — Phase 0 builds, with zero @jwildfire time: a fit-gap matrix with a blank "target" column for him to fill, a numbered as-is/to-be workflow walkthrough to red-line, three divergent goal drafts (different governing principles, presented side by side), and latent requirements mined from the program record with verbatim source quotes, offered accept/reject/edit. He corrects; he does not compose.
  • grilling-style engine — design tree + frontier; recommended answers on convergence questions (decide-by-exception, the thing that demonstrably gets answered); facts looked up by agents, never asked; open-generative turns before any menu, his unprompted answer recorded before agent hypotheses are revealed; laddering, critical incidents, teach-a-novice, repertory-grid triads, and a premortem rotated by topic; at least one forced ranking/cut with no draft position, and boundary/ownership questions asked explicitly (the CSR holes).
  • wayfinder-style persistence — a hub-side ledger (reports/goal-79-elicitation/: log.md, answers.json, frontier.md with fog-of-war, published index) committed after every answer batch; one topic per 15–25-minute session; resumable any day; wrap phase drafts the goal prose from decided entries with bidirectional question-ID ↔ issue traceability, and closes A3/A4 on their artifact and Q&A thread.

Honest time budget: Phase 1 ≈ 30 min; then 2–4 sessions of 15–25 min; wrap review ≈ 20 min. Total ≈ 1.5–2.5 hours of his attention across 4–6 short sittings. The research says one pass misses ~25% even for a good LLM interviewer — the fog-of-war ledger is the design admission of that, not a failure of it.

The four calls (E1–E4)

Each has a recommendation baked into the shipped skill as the default. Answering "defaults" in one line is a complete response; any pick amends the skill.

E1 — Interview mode · Recommended: (b) reactive-first hybrid

a
Pure grill Q&A (Pocock as-is): cheapest to run, but blank-page-shaped — the evidence says it loses most of the tacit content, and it is single-session by design.
b
Reactive-first hybrid (default): artifacts to correct, then targeted grill rounds. Costs agent prep time (free to you); forecloses nothing.
c
Pure CSR-style proposal + decide-by-exception, no interview: your demonstrated lowest-cost pattern, but it only surfaces tacit content by luck (an objection you happen to volunteer) — it is how hub#131 nearly didn't happen.

E2 — Question cadence · Recommended: (b) small rounds, one topic per session

a
Strictly one question at a time: best anti-anchoring hygiene, slowest; multiplies sittings.
b
AskUserQuestion rounds of ≤4 independent questions, one topic per 15–25-min session (default): matches your stated inline-multiple-choice preference and phone-sized replies.
c
Full-frontier rounds (Pocock default, ~46 questions/session): efficient for a sit-down marathon you have said you won't do.

E3 — Where the ledger lives · Recommended: (a) hub reports/ folder

a
obot.roadmap/reports/goal-79-elicitation/ (default): direct-commit-to-main grant makes per-answer commits free; published to Pages so you can review state in Chrome; git history is the audit trail.
b
obot.agent/interviews/: matches the old convention, but every write is a PR through obot-merge — friction that pushes the agent to batch writes, which is the evaporation failure mode.
c
Wayfinder-literal: one hub issue per open question. Maximum visibility, but floods the issue list and the goalless-audit with ~dozens of non-requirement issues.

E4 — How answers reach goal #79's body · Recommended: (a) grant asked up front

a
Ask for the body-edit grant as question zero of Phase 1 (default): on Approve at wrap, the agent applies the ratified Intent/Boundaries under the documented exception route (strikethrough, exception recorded in a comment). Keeps the exercise from stalling on your editing time.
b
Proposed-body comment only, you apply by hand: maximally conservative, and the known way elicitation output sits in a comment for weeks.

What unblocks

  • On "defaults" (or silence): any session runs /grill-me 79 --prep; the Phase-0 artifacts get built and published; you start Phase 1 whenever you have ~30 minutes. Wrap closes A3/A4 and produces the ratified #79 body + requirements — which then unblocks executing the accepted A1/A2.
  • On any E-pick: the skill is amended first (one small PR on the standard lane), then the same path.
  • Not gated on this: A1/A2 execution order — but running the interview first is recommended, since the A2 requirement texts should cite the elicitation ledger.
