THE ANSWER

Is the framework good enough to push towards v1.0?

Recommendation

Yes for building clinical work, and no for calling anything 1.0 — and those are two different problems, only one of which is about the framework. The machine that builds has spent the last month proving it can do clinical work well: it delivered a kidney-safety explorer, a Kaplan–Meier survival chart whose estimator agrees with R’s survival package to ten decimal places, three participant-level safety metrics, and a study census, each through the same lane — isolated branch, tests written first, a published evidence page, release notes. Two of those were displays last month’s survey listed as things everyone else ships and we did not. What is not ready is the bookkeeping a version number rests on: nobody has ever written down what 1.0 contains, the design question that would let the app’s requirements be filed at all has sat open since 12 July, and the demo study a 1.0 would be judged on has failed its weekly rebuild three times running since 3 August, and the issue filed for it was never ranked. Push, and start this weekend with clinical build work — but the first hour of it belongs to those three, because they cost almost nothing and every week of a v1.0 push without them arrives at a version nobody can define.

The two readings of “framework”, because they answer differently

You used the word twice in one breath — “how some clinical build work goes in our new framework”, then “whether the current framework is good enough”. Read as the machine that builds — workers, lanes, gates, release candidates, evidence pages — the answer is yes, with one caveat below. Read as the product architecture — the chart library, the R package that carries it into clinical reports, the app that assembles them — the answer is yes for the charts and not yet for the layer around them. Both readings are answered on this page; the first is the one the recommendation above is about.

What the machine has actually done with clinical work

  • Two charts the survey listed as missing. The kidney explorer landed on 14 August, the Kaplan–Meier time-to-event chart on 15 August. The second was on the survey’s headline list of things at least four competing platforms ship and we did not.
  • Correct conduct where the statistics were beyond its authority. The survival chart was built with six statistical decisions an unattended session could not get signed — which estimator, how the bands are labelled, what the risk table means. It adopted them provisionally, shipped the chart marked Experimental, said so in the app and in the clinical guide, and filed the requirement to remove that marking after a clinician reviews them. That is the behaviour you would want; the review has not happened and the requirement is sitting in the backlog.
  • A clinical claim measured before it was made. The three participant-level safety metrics were run on the demo data and the counts published before the metrics were treated as real.
  • Clinical definitions kept upstream. The death count in the census rebuild you approved uses the standard mapping’s existing definition rather than inventing one.
The caveat, and it is the honest one

Of the fifty-seven items this program closed in August, seven carry the safety label, and six of those seven were closed on a single day in what reads as bookkeeping on work that shipped weeks earlier. The machine is capable of clinical work. It has mostly not been pointed at it. That is what you are correcting, and it is a scheduling fact rather than a capability one.

What is not ready, in four sentences

  • No written definition of 1.0. The question that would have — the third question on the app-plan page from 14 August, “what is in v1.0 and what is deferred, in writing” — was never answered, and you closed the page with it open. It moved to the goal-79 interview, which has not been run.
  • The gate on filing any app requirement. The design discussion about what GitHub is to the app has been open since 12 July, in the backlog. It explicitly blocks filing the app’s phase requirements. Meanwhile the app shipped a local-first engine in its unreleased 0.2.0 — which is the architecture that discussion was called to settle, and the one you said you did not agree with.
  • A demo study that does not rebuild. Its weekly pipeline has failed on 3, 10 and 17 August, every time at the step that installs its pinned packages. The site is still up on the last good run. A push towards 1.0 that starts on a red demo starts on sand, and the issue for it was filed and never ranked.
  • A wrong liver chart in the R package. Details in the ten below; it is the first item there for a reason.

The chart count, stated honestly

Thirteen of the thirty chart types are shipped. Of the seventeen that are not: four are already filed as requirements, four are marked low priority by the survey itself and I agree with all four, and nine are genuinely wanted and unfiled. Both sentences are worth having, because the first is what a September audience will see and the second is what a build plan has to work from.

Where I disagree with the reading you were given

The summary reaching you was that charts are nearly done, the workflow layer was never started, and therefore the gap is the platform around the charts rather than the charts. That is right as strategy and wrong in two particulars, and one of the particulars changes what to do this weekend.

  • The zero-filed line. Out of date — the review layer has a requirement, written at your direction, with the hard part — where mutable review state lives when the whole data architecture rests on immutable snapshots — already identified as its central design problem. The layer is unstarted, not unnoticed.
  • The nearly-done claim. It overstates the position by row count: nine wanted charts are unshipped and unfiled, and they cluster into exactly two families rather than a long tail: comparison-with-an-interval, and cross-domain context.
  • Right quarter, wrong weekend. The two most valuable clinical things available right now are a chart family you approved last night and a chart the R package is drawing wrongly today. Telling you the gap is the platform is correct, and doing platform work first this weekend is not.

WHAT DOES NOT EXIST YET

Five things worth ranking that have no issue to rank

None of these can go on a ranked list, because ranking a thing with no requirement behind it is how work goes missing. Each needs filing first. I checked both trackers by search rather than by memory; nothing matching any of these exists anywhere.

chart · the strongest genuine hole

Comparing arms with an interval — the risk-difference screen

Two linked views of one question: which adverse-event terms differ between treatment arms and by how much. A volcano for screening hundreds of terms at once, and a paired panel — incidence per arm on the left, the difference with a confidence interval on the right — for reading the top of that list. We count adverse events and we list them and we never compare arms with an interval, which is the first question anyone asks of an imbalance. The original working group already built one and it was never migrated. Two of the FDA’s core figures are the static twin.

chart · the cheapest genuine hole

Mean change from baseline over time, with confidence intervals

Our results-over-time chart draws quantile boxes, which answer “how spread out is this”. Every platform surveyed also draws the mean with an interval at each visit, which answers “did the group move”, and reviewers use both. It reuses the existing chart’s data contract unchanged, which is what makes it the cheapest real gap on the list. Two more FDA core figures.

charts · every platform opens here

The context set — demographics, disposition, enrolment

The views a reviewer opens before any safety display: baseline characteristics by arm, discontinuations and their reasons, enrolment over time by site. Five platforms open on these and we ship none of them. Without them a reviewer cannot answer the standard first challenge to anything else we draw — is this a safety signal or a population difference.

platform · regressed

One filter and one selection, across charts

Pick a participant in one chart and every open chart follows; set a filter once and it holds everywhere. Five platforms do the first, seven the second. The requirement that would have carried this closed on 15 August with the mechanism unbuilt, so this capability now has no issue at all — it went from filed to unfiled without anyone deciding to drop it. A searchable participant selector is filed in the chart library, but that is a component, not the mechanism.

platform · the survey’s broadest finding

What changed since my last review, per participant

The app has change chips between snapshots at study and site level. What does not exist is the participant-level version, and the part that makes it a review tool rather than a diff: scoping it to when this reviewer last looked. It is inside the action-log requirement’s scope, so this may be a design decision inside that rather than a sixth filing — worth saying which.

One piece of bookkeeping, while you are here

The abnormal-baseline liver tooling requirement has all three of its sub-issues closed and is still open. It looks finished. Somebody should read it and close it rather than leave it counting against the quarter.

CHARTS TO MAKE

The charts, ranked by what they answer clinically

You asked directly, so this section is by clinical value rather than by how many competitors ship the thing. The count of platforms is in the survey and it is a weak signal on its own — every platform ships demographics tables, and that does not make them the most valuable thing we could build next.

Cheap, given what the library already has

  • Mean change from baseline with confidence intervals. Answers “did the group move”, which our quantile boxes cannot. Reuses the results-over-time data contract without changing it, so the cost is the drawing and the statistics language, not a new pipeline. Small. Unfiled.
  • Data coverage per visit. Answers whether a quiet visit is reassuring or empty. The number already exists in the census work; the chart is the presentation. Small. Filed.
  • Graded abnormality shift. Severity grading is how lab abnormalities are actually reviewed, and no renderer takes a grade column today — so this is a data-contract addition plus a variant of the shift plot we already draw. Small to medium. Unfiled, and the survey defers it to the internal atlas rather than proposing it.

Not cheap, and the highest clinical value

  • Risk-difference screening across adverse-event terms. The first question of any imbalance, and we cannot answer it. Needs a defensible statistical choice and language that says screening rather than inference. Medium. Unfiled.
  • One participant, every domain, one axis. The question a liver signal always raises — what else was happening and what were they taking. Our event timeline is adverse-events-only and the participant profile is labs-only, and concomitant medications and medical history are the specific domains absent. The original chart suite shipped both and neither was in the migration set. Medium. Partly filed as an exposure track inside the liver explorer; the timeline itself is not.
  • The context set. Demographics, disposition, enrolment. Medium. Unfiled.

Deliberately not, and worth writing down as such

  • Tendril plots. Low value, and the package we are putting back on CRAN is removing its tendril chart to get there.
  • Correlation heatmaps, scatter-plot matrices, response curves. Biomarker and efficacy exploration rather than safety review.
  • Geographic site maps. Striking in a demo and thin in use.
  • The data-integrity family. Digit preference, duplicate subjects, implausible sequences. It is the largest single block we do not touch and it is arguably a different product — closer to the risk-indicator engine than to the chart library — and it is the one place where a badly worded display accuses a site of misconduct. Worth a scoping requirement, not a build.
