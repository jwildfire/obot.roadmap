The session model after obot-prime

You said it yourself last night: the standing prime breaks the session model. Every bookend assumes a session that starts and ends — prime does neither, so init has nothing to open, the wrapup has nothing to fire on, and the concepts underneath them each need a verdict rather than a vibe. You also gave a provisional lean — a tight daily briefing with headlines and todos, maybe audio, plus a detailed weekly — and asked for honest suggestions. This page gives a verdict per concept (M1), a trigger model for the wrapup's orphaned duties (M2), a briefing designed from the measured reasons the openclaw one failed (M3), the weekly (M4), and the audio question researched properly (M5).

The situation

Three sentences

The evidence

Why the openclaw summaries died — measured, not remembered

The corpus survives: 32 entries, 2026-05-11 → 06-11, in obot-claw.github.io/daily/ (mirrored into this hub's diary). 18,760 words total, ~570 per entry. The git log shows an unconditional cron committing at 23:31–23:35 ET every night, including zero-activity days, to a Pages site you had to navigate to, with no notification lane of any kind.

SpecimenWordsWhere the ask sat
2026-05-12136No ask anywhere — narrative recap, 2 bare URLs
2026-05-25559No ask — a self-described quiet day: 559 words to say nothing changed, re-listing 8 unchanged draft PRs
2026-06-088657 real decision asks — starting at line 38, after ~500 words of recap

The decision-asks section (🙋 ToDo) existed in only the last 7 of 32 entries; word count grew monotonically 136 → 865 as template sections accreted. Three failure causes, each with a design consequence:

  • It recapped a day you lived, after it ended. Published at the one hour you weren't working, up to ~23h stale, and unconditionally — quiet days still produced 500+ words, which trained you that opening it usually paid nothing. Consequence: gate on content; never publish filler.
  • The asks were absent or buried. No decision section at all for the first 25 of 32 entries; once added, it sat fourth of six sections, below ~500 words of recap. The one thing that needed your eyes never led. Consequence: the briefing IS the ask list; the record lives elsewhere.
  • It was an errand shaped like a document. A separate site trip to a fixed six-section template whose length tracked the template, not the signal, and nothing gated on reading it — every ask was also on GitHub. Consequence: push a one-liner to the phone; make skipping it free.

What the news feed you like does differently

The dashboard's "Roadmap activity" panel (session-hub/lib/render.mjs): one line per event, ~15–20 words, every line linked, verb bolded (12:02 AM · obot.agent · PR #91 … — merged); a 5-minute sweep TTL and 60-second refresh, so items appear minutes after they happen; a collapsed panel showing "N events" inside a tab you already have open — reading it is a glance, not an errand; and it never editorializes, because curation lives in the Priorities panel beside it. Every one of those properties is the inverse of a diagnosed failure above. M3 inherits all of them.

M2 — the trigger

What fires the wrapup's duties when nothing ends

The wrapup was never one thing — it bundled five duties (clean roadmap, everything captured, scaffold reviewed, summary posted, next session prepped), and the session boundary was merely a convenient moment to do all five at once. With the boundary gone, each duty needs its own trigger. The models on the table, and what each produces:

T1 — Wall-clock, unconditional (the openclaw model)

Proven failure

A cron fires nightly and publishes whatever exists. Produces a complete, evenly-spaced record — and the measured behavior above: 559 words on a quiet day, asks at line 38, a reader trained to skip. The record was fine; the reading died. Rejected as the sole model, kept only as the clock inside the recommendation.

T2 — Pure event-based

Fragments

Fold whenever a defined event lands: an RC files, a decision is answered, N delegates close out, a release ships. Produces maximally fresh, minimally batched output — and a day of drips: five small folds on a busy day is five interruptions, and the diary shatters into fragments no keynote can quote. Right for the two genuinely urgent event classes, wrong for the record.

T3 — Threshold-based

Unpredictable

Fold when unreported events accumulate past a bound (say, 15 scratchpad lines, or any RC unreported >12h). Produces output proportional to activity — at unpredictable times, which is exactly wrong for a habit: a briefing that arrives at 07:00 some days and 15:40 others never becomes "the thing I glance at over coffee". The gate is right; the clock is wrong.

T4 — On-demand only

No record

Nothing fires; you ask prime when you want the picture. Produces zero unread artifacts — and zero record: the diary (and the keynote diary with it) stops accruing, hygiene never runs, and the queue is only as visible as your memory to ask. This is what exists by default since prime launched, and it is why last night needed a milestone audit to reconstruct a release.

T5 — Composite: content-gated morning fold + event push + event-time hygiene

Recommended

Split the duties by their real latency needs:

07:00 fold
A cron-spawned 👯🤖 fold sibling (independent of prime, so the record survives prime's retirement) reads the scratchpads since the last fold + the gh sweep, then — only if there is content — writes the diary entry for a day with activity, renders the briefing (M3), runs the inbox backstop sweep, and checks off captured items. A zero-activity night produces nothing at all: no page, no push, no filler. It fires at 07:00, not 23:30 — the briefing describes the queue you're waking to, not the day you already lived.
Event push
Exactly two classes interrupt you, immediately, via a one-line phone push (the harness's PushNotification reaches desktop + phone over Remote Control, which prime and siblings hold; verify the bg-job path at build time): an RC goes ready with its demo, and every active goal is blocked at once (the escalation case rc-framework already names). Everything else waits for the fold.
Hygiene
Moves to event time, into the shipping sibling's definition of done — milestones at pickup (your rule, already live), Closes lines, stage moves, close-out heartbeat lines. Most of this migrated already; M2 just names it as the wrapup's successor instead of a nightly batch repair.
Verification
Navigator, #157 — decided last night, not re-decided here. Until it exists, the fold does a light verify (URLs 200, claimed PRs real) inherited from wrapup-verify.
Hand-off
Already continuous: prime-state.md + the scratchpad. No fold step needed.
M2 — Recommended

T5: a content-gated 07:00 fold for the record and the briefing, push for the two urgent event classes, hygiene at event time, Navigator for truth.

This is the wall-clock's predictability with the threshold's gate and the event model's urgency, each applied only where it wins. A standing prime (or any cron) can hold the clock and push — the thing no previous session could do — and the fold sibling is deliberately not prime, so the record never depends on one long-lived session staying alive.

M3 — the daily briefing

Headlines and todos — designed from the autopsy

Your lean, taken seriously. Five rules, each the inverse of a measured failure:

  • State-shaped, not delta-shaped. The briefing is today's queue — the cumulative 🚦 RCs and 🧭 decisions (the wrapup's two headlines, which already carry until you close them) plus the 🙋 mechanical todos. Skip a week and the next briefing is still complete; the openclaw summaries were newspapers, where a missed day was gone. This is the single most important property: the design must survive you not reading it, because that is the documented failure mode.
  • ≤ 10 lines, every line linked, asks first. News-feed idiom: one line per item, ~15–20 words, the ask or verb up front. The record (what shipped overnight) is one trailing line, not the body — the diary holds the prose.
  • Content-gated. Empty queue + no overnight events → no briefing, no push. Silence means "nothing needs you", and it stays credible only if it is true.
  • Pushed, with a free skip. One PushNotification line ("1 RC · 3 decisions — briefing →") linking one stable URL — reports/briefing/ on the hub, always the current state, phone-bookmarkable. No dated pages to catch up on; the diary is the archive.
  • Composed mechanically. Bullets derive from prime-state's Open section, the gh sweep, and the scratchpad — the same union the wrapup headlines already use. The model writes nothing but the overnight line, so the briefing cannot bloat the way the 136→865-word template did.

M3 — Recommended

Build this as the rc-framework's "nightly executive summary", replacing that spec's five-section page with the two-queue shape above.

Amend rc-framework.md on approval rather than shipping a competitor to an unbuilt spec. Build cost is small — the fold composes from surfaces that already exist (prime-state, gh-sweep, scratchpad, the cumulative headline rules) — roughly one evening increment, filed as a hub requirement (2026q3) through the normal lifecycle once you answer.

What you have to do to keep it alive — stated plainly

  • Minimum: nothing. The queue is cumulative and the push is skippable; ignoring it for a week costs nothing and the briefing does not degrade. That is deliberate — your openclaw experience says "read a thing every morning" is exactly the commitment that fails.
  • For it to be useful: one tap on the push, ~20 seconds, on mornings you have them. The bet is that shrinking the read from a 570-word site errand to a 9-line pushed card moves it under your threshold — and if after three weeks the pushes go untapped, the honest response is to kill the push and keep the fold (the record still matters for the keynote), not to make the briefing longer.
M4 — the weekly

The detailed weekly briefing

Recommended, with one job: be the sink that keeps the daily at 9 lines. A Sunday-evening fold (same machinery, weekly flavor) composes from the week's daily folds: goal-by-goal progress against the keynote plan, the scaffold-candidate sweep (re-homed from wrapup step 6), the cost/usage note, carried-item staleness ("hub#140 is now 20 days open"), and a short week narrative — which is, not incidentally, the R/Pharma diary's raw material. It publishes as a dated hub page and one push line. Unlike the daily it is not gated to nothing — a quiet week is itself a finding — but it skips sections rather than padding them; the 136→865-word template creep is the failure mode it must never repeat. If you read one artifact a week, this is the one; if you read neither, the keynote diary still accrues.

M4 — Recommended

Yes — weekly detail, Sunday evening, built by the same fold machinery; the daily stays headlines-and-todos only.

Ship it in the same requirement as M3 but a phase later: the weekly needs daily folds to fold, so it cannot come first.

M5 — audio

Audio, researched rather than hand-waved

The facts, web-verified 2026-08-15 (full research with source URLs: sources/audio-research.md):

  • Spotify: no. A listener still cannot add an arbitrary or private RSS URL to Spotify — no such setting exists; it remains an open community feature request, and every current guide routes private feeds to other apps. Your half-memory is almost certainly Spotify's paid-podcast private feeds, which flow the other direction (out of Spotify into other apps). The only road in is public-catalog submission — wrong for a personal briefing.
  • Apple Podcasts: yes. Library → "…" → Follow a Show by URL takes a plain unlisted RSS URL; one-time setup, then episodes auto-download like any show (CarPlay, watch, speed controls). Overcast and Pocket Casts likewise.
  • Hosting: trivial. A static feed.xml + mp3s on the existing Pages deploy; a 2–3 min briefing ≈ 1–1.5 MB/day, ~0.5 GB/yr — inside limits with the feed pruned to the last ~60 episodes. (A tiny dedicated repo would keep mp3 blobs out of the hub's git history.)
  • Voice: ~$1/month. OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts at ~$0.03/briefing is the pick; macOS say (Ava Premium, already installed) is the $0-but-flat fallback; Kokoro-82M local is the $0 upgrade path; ElevenLabs needs $22/mo for daily use — overkill.
  • Build: ~4–5 hours for the full pipeline (TTS call → mp3 → RSS regen → deploy → one-time phone follow). A ~1-hour, $0 alternative skips all infrastructure: an iOS Shortcut that speaks the briefing text on "Hey Siri, obot briefing" — it tests the actual listen-over-coffee hypothesis with nothing to maintain.
M5 — Recommended

Later, gated: run the text briefing for 2–3 weeks first; build the podcast pipeline only if you're actually reading it and want it in your ears instead.

Every technical question resolves cleanly — the risk is purely audience. Audio is a renderer for a habit that does not exist yet, and a podcast queue of unplayed episodes is the ignored-summary problem with extra infrastructure. If you want to test audio now, take the 1-hour Shortcut, not the 5-hour pipeline; its usage decides whether the pipeline is ever worth building. And since your listening life may be Spotify-centric: that is an argument for the Shortcut/HomePod lane, because a private feed can never reach Spotify.
