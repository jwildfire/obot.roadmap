safety.viz release review 2026-10-02

What v1.8.0 adds, annotated

safety.viz becomes something you can use on your own study. A demo app puts every chart on one study, says which ones your data can feed, and lets you load your own files and map their columns, all in the browser. A fourteenth renderer follows one participant through a trial. And nine requests from the people who used the original renderers land on the existing charts. Every capture below was taken from the live dev site, which is the release-candidate build.

The demo app

The docs site keeps its gallery and its per-chart demos. The app is a page of its own, with its own header and footer, so the chart has the full width of the screen. The header has a tab for your data and a tab for each data domain, saying how many of that domain’s charts the loaded data supports. Beneath the tabs, on one line, are the open domain’s charts.

  • A filled hex means the chart is ready. A red one means something it needs is missing, and the chip says how many things. A hollow one means no file is loaded for it.
  • On the demo study thirteen of the fourteen charts are ready. The Patient Journey Explorer sits under Other, reading “needs more domains”: it takes six domains of its own that the four standard ones do not supply.
  • The four standard domains, their columns, and what each chart needs from them are on the new Domains page, generated from one manifest that a test holds to every chart’s own schema.

Why it matters

The original safetyGraphics app put a study’s charts in one place and told you which ones your data could feed. safety.viz had fourteen separate demo pages and no shared statement of what data a study supplies. This is that app again, running entirely in the browser.

Try it

Open the demo app
  1. Click the ECG tab: it opens straight onto the QT explorer.
  2. Click Labs and vitals, then Hepatic Explorer in the row beneath.
  3. Click a point on the scatter: the participant profile opens beside the chart.
  4. Click Other: the Patient Journey Explorer says why it is not drawn here.

Load your own study

Drop CSV or JSON files on the Data view. Each file is read in the browser and placed in a domain by its column names, with the number of that domain’s columns it carries. A test asserts that no network request leaves the page from the first file onward.

Two rules to confirm

  • Which measures each chart cannot draw without (ALT and total bilirubin for the hepatic explorer, creatinine for the kidney explorer, one QTc correction for the QT explorer) is read from the charts’ code, not from a reviewed clinical rule.
  • When a labs file has no study-day column, the hepatic charts and the participant profile are given visit order instead, as the site’s own demo pages do.

Why it matters

A study programmer has a folder of extracts and wants to see them in the charts without installing anything or sending data anywhere. The old app did this in R. Here the files are read with the browser’s own file API, and the single file needs no server at all.

A guess is always labelled as a guess, and a gap is priced in the charts it turns off, by name. The mapping downloads as a small JSON file, and choosing that file again with your data restores it.

Try it

Open the Data view
  1. Save these four files: labs_final.csv, dm.csv, ae.csv, ecg.json.
  2. Drop them on the drop zone. The header drops to 7 of 14 charts across its tabs.
  3. Under labs_final.csv set Upper limit of normal to ULN, Treatment arm to TREATMENT, and Total bilirubin to Tot. Bilirubin.
  4. Set Treatment arm to TREATMENT under ae.csv and ecg.json, and End-of-study day to LASTDAY under dm.csv.
  5. Open Labs and vitals, then Hepatic Explorer.
  6. In the footer, choose Download as one file, open it from your disk, and do the same there.

Patient Journey Explorer

Experimental

The fourteenth renderer, and the first that looks at one participant and not across them: exposure, dose changes, adverse events, labs, concomitant medications, medical history and disposition as stacked lanes on one study-day axis. Clicking any mark anchors time on it and lists what was recorded around it. The chart can also draft a narrative of what the record shows, every sentence carrying the rows it came from, marked as a draft until a reviewer accepts it.

Why it matters

Reviewing a single participant usually means opening five listings and lining the days up by eye. This puts them on one axis, and makes the question “what else was happening then” one click.

The narrative layer is the program’s first AI feature. Its rules are the product: grounded in the rows on screen, cited sentence by sentence, never published without a reviewer’s accept.

Try it

Open the Patient Journey Explorer
  1. Pick a participant from the list on the left.
  2. Click an adverse-event bar: the panel beside the chart lists the labs, medications and dose changes around it.
  3. Choose Show full narrative, and click a citation to see the row it points to.
  4. Read the evidence page: each requirement row traces to a named test.

Nine requests from the original renderers’ users

When the RhoInc and SafetyGraphics renderers were retired they left 282 open issues behind. A survey of all twelve trackers found 144 still worth carrying over and picked nine that the shared modules made cheap. All nine are in this release.

  • A filter means the same thing in every chart: it can open on a value, drop its All option, or take several values at once.
  • A measures setting chooses which measures the Measure control offers, and in what order.
  • A Reset chart control on nine charts returns filters, measures, visits, scales and selections to how the chart opened.
  • The Adverse Event Explorer says what kind of empty an empty table is.
  • The Shift Plot learns a log scale.
  • The QT explorer prints its confidence intervals as a table, and carries its safety language permanently.
  • The Hepatic Safety Explorer can include or exclude unscheduled visits, and re-derives baseline and peak when it does.
  • Fixed: the Adverse Event Explorer drew no counts for a single-arm study with the per-group columns switched off.

Why it matters

These were filed by the clinicians, programmers and reviewers who ran the original charts on studies, some as long ago as 2016. The release notes name the issue each one answers and, where the tracker recorded it, the person who filed it.

Try it

Open the Shift Plot
  1. Set Measure to Alkaline Phosphatase and Axis Type to log.
  2. Scroll to the foot of the controls and choose Reset chart.
  3. Open the QT explorer and read the table under the chart.
before you approve

What is not in this release

  • One set of arm, site and population filters across every chart, and a saved study configuration.
  • The participant profile opening from every chart on the mapped identifier.
  • Detecting the standard, not only the domain, of a file.
  • A demo study inlined in the single file, and a size budget for it.
  • The Patient Journey Explorer inside the demo app: the manifest does not name its six domains yet.

Where to look further

  • The release notes, as they will publish.
  • The demo app’s design, and its requirement matrix.
  • The manifest the app and the Domains page read.
