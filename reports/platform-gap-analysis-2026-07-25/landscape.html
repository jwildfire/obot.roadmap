The landscape

Thirteen platforms a medical monitor or safety reviewer might actually be sitting in front of, plus the two published catalogues several of them implement. Each capsule is what the tool is, what it is known for, and where that was read.

Capsules record what the source documents say, with the distinctive items in bold — the things that turn up on the gap matrix. Where a vendor publishes little, the capsule is short rather than padded out with marketing language; that asymmetry is in the sources, not a judgment about the products.

Reading the survey

Three patterns are worth naming before the matrix.

  • The open-source tools compete on modules; the commercial tools compete on workflow. teal, DaVinci and tidyCDISC lead with how many analyses they offer. JReview, elluminate, CluePoints and Empirica lead with what happens around the analysis — who reviewed what, what changed, what was raised and how it closed. This portfolio has been competing in the first race and is not entered in the second.
  • The patient profile is the universal object. Ten of thirteen platforms have one, and it is usually the destination of every other display. Ours shipped in July; hub#75 is already growing it. The survey’s message is that the growth direction should be more domains, not more lab detail — every other platform’s profile spans AE, CM, exposure, medical history and visits.
  • Nobody has solved the hepatic abnormal-baseline case. Five platforms ship an eDISH plot. None ships the baseline-referenced composite or the ALT waterfall for populations enrolled with elevated baseline liver tests, which is exactly where eDISH’s quadrants stop meaning anything. That is a real lead, published in 2024 and 2025 and implemented here within months.
