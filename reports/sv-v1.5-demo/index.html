sv#105 · hub#45

Participant profile

Experimental

Drilling into one person used to mean whatever detail panel a given renderer happened to have. v1.5.0 replaces that with a single chart-agnostic module — demographics header, labs-over-time spaghetti, per-measure table — that any chart can hand a participant to. Six lab renderers adopted it in this release: hep-explorer, shift-plot, outlier-explorer, delta-delta, histogram and qt-explorer. sv#112 then added a second data domain — adverse events — which let ae-explorer and ae-timelines adopt it too.

This section shows profile v2. sv#112 merged to dev on the evening of 25 July, and its D4 removes the dock: the profile now opens in a right-hand rail beside the chart, gains an expand state, and reads adverse events as well as labs. The captures below were re-taken against that build. The module itself and the participantsSelected contract are unchanged — only the surfacing and the second domain are new.

Click a point in one chart, read the whole lab course in another

The two modules in this clip share no internals. The scatter dispatches participantsSelected; the standalone profile below it is listening. That event is the contract — and the part of this feature that is stable.

What the profile shows

Where it opens: the rail, beside the chart

The second domain: adverse events on the labs chart's own axis

Why it matters

Every lab chart now answers “who is that point?” the same way, so a reviewer learns one surface instead of six. It also unblocks the charts that never had a drill-down at all.

It ships Experimental on purpose, and that licence has already been spent once: hub#75 — the rail, the expand state and the adverse-event domain — landed inside this same release as sv#112 rather than after it, which is why the surface here is not the one the earlier PRs shipped. If you wire the profile into your own chart, wire the event, not the surface.

Try it

Open the Hepatic Safety Explorer
  1. Click any point in the scatter — the further right and up, the more interesting the case. The rail opens beside it.
  2. Press Expand in the rail head, then Escape to come back.
  3. Click a sparkline in the measure table to expand it into a full chart.
  4. Drag a box across several points: the rail head becomes a stepper, worst case first.

For adverse events, open the participant-profile demo instead — it is the one wired with an AE dataset. Note that the crafted CLD-* Hy's-Law cases carry no AE records, so pick a point outside that cluster to see the block populated.

sv#97 · hub#43

Migration Sankey and the ALT waterfall

Prototypes

Two tools for trials that enrol participants whose liver tests are already abnormal — chronic liver disease, NASH, hepatitis. In those studies the eDISH ×ULN quadrants fill with people who were there on day one, and the scatter stops discriminating. Both come from Amirzadegan et al., Drug Safety 2025;48:443–453.

Mirrored about a pinned column of baseline quadrants

And built for one gesture: hand a group of participants to the composite plot

The waterfall puts the axis back in absolute U/L

Why it matters

These are the first two displays in the portfolio aimed squarely at abnormal-baseline populations, where the standard eDISH read is misleading rather than merely noisy.

Both are marked prototype in the gallery: the clinical judgement calls in design #43 are still open, so behaviour and settings can move before they are called finished.

Try it

Open hep-explorer, then switch View to Migration
  1. Hover a red ribbon: it names the shift and the count.
  2. Click it, then take the “Review these N” hand-off.
  3. Then open the ALT waterfall and hover across the seam.
sv#110 · hub#88

The eDISH scatter gets its hands back

Four sets of features deferred from the v1.3 coordinated-core port land together: direct manipulation of the cut-lines, marginal distributions, the chart saying what it means, and traceable data handling.

Drag a Hy's-Law cut-line and everything reclassifies under your hand

Marginal box plots and rugs, following the filtered view

The chart now says what it means

Data handling you can audit

The waterfall's flanking panels and bars now answer a hover

Why it matters

The cut-lines are the argument the eDISH scatter makes. Being able to move them and watch the population reclassify is how a reviewer tests whether a signal is real or an artefact of where the line happened to sit.

Six of the eight #88 gaps close here. Still open and not in v1.5.0: transition animation (sv#46), listing sparklines (sv#48) and the exposure track (sv#49, needs adex).

Try it

Open hep-explorer on the eDISH view
  1. Drag the vertical dashed line right, past the cluster near ALT 4.
  2. Watch the Possible Hy's Law row in the table, and the ALT Reference Line box.
  3. Set Marginal Distributions to Hidden and back, then filter to one treatment group.
sv#108 · sv#85

Axis-limit boxes load showing the limits in force

The limit inputs used to sit empty until you typed into them, which made auto and override look identical. They now load carrying the data-driven values the chart actually resolved, so you can see the current limits and nudge one without having to supply both.

Why it matters

Small, but it removes a class of confusion: an empty box used to mean both “auto” and “I have not touched this”, and typing one limit against a blank other could send an inverted domain to the chart. The invert guard now reads the displayed value, so a crossing entry swaps the pair instead of breaking the axis.

Try it

Open the outlier explorer
  1. Read the Y-axis Limits boxes before touching anything.
  2. Type a Lower above the Upper shown and tab out.
  3. Change the measure and watch both boxes re-derive.
notes

Reading this page

How the captures were made

  • Playwright drove Chromium against the live dev site — the same build this release promotes — on 2026-07-25. Nothing was staged or mocked. The profile section's captures were taken twice: first that afternoon, then again late that evening against the rail, after sv#112 merged and removed the dock. Every other capture predates sv#112 and shows behaviour it did not touch.
  • Clips are silent WebM at 7–8 fps, 4–5 s, captured frame by frame during the real interaction. Stills are 2× PNG-quality JPEG.
  • The demo data is the shared adbds cohort: 318 participants for the eDISH views, 58 for the abnormal-baseline waterfall. The adverse-event demo reads adae, which covers 254 of those 318 — but none of the 24 crafted CLD-* Hy's-Law cases, so those participants' profiles report no events. That is a gap in the demo fixture, not in the module.

Also in the release, not shown here

  • hep-core — the 2,149-line hep-explorer splits into an orchestrator plus per-view components. One behaviour change fell out: peaks now exclude the baseline record by identity rather than by study day, which had been counting 24 participants' own baselines as on-treatment.
  • Requirement matrices moved in-repo (sv#111), so a chart's spec, tests and evidence now move in one PR.
  • Closed-PR preview sites redirect to the dev tier instead of 404-ing; dist/safety.viz-1.4.1/ is restored to the bytes v1.4.1 shipped.
