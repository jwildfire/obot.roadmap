## Work completed

**The design record, and what it decided.** Goal #79 had no design artifact of any kind, so the session produced three. A [roadmap-state briefing](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/22e57954-56b9-4fb4-8441-5effea89978f) established the honest picture: direction fully decided since 19 July, deliverable never written. Then [**Part 1 — design directions**](https://jwildfire.github.io/obot.roadmap/reports/app-design-2026-07-28/directions.html) put three shells on the table (Monitor, Gallery, Study site) and recommended the study-site frame, with @jwildfire's **Domain** concept — RBQM and Safety as first-class lenses over one study — making the graft structural rather than cosmetic; the provenance chip emerged as the signature component, since nothing in the ecosystem composes snapshot id, data version, package pins and run status even though every ingredient exists. [**Part 2 — the data & config framework**](https://jwildfire.github.io/obot.roadmap/reports/app-design-2026-07-28/framework.html) stated the substrate: workr pipelines, config-as-code in a forkable study repo, Project Snapshots as the only data interface, a Domain as a config entry rather than code. Its load-bearing finding, read from source: **an `og_run()` project folder and a published snapshot branch are the same shape** — local runs and Actions runs produce byte-compatible trees, so the app cannot tell them apart. Sixteen decisions (D-APP0–7, D-FW1–8), approved in session.

**demo-301 v0 — built, published, verified ([#134](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/134)).** Two ultracode workflows, nine Opus agents, an independent verifier at each stage. Stage one landed the design record on the hub, **merged open.gismo PR #1 after sixteen days as a draft** (the feared conflict-heavy merge turned out to be a clean fast-forward — `dev` had never diverged), scaffolded the study repo with its Domain registry and 53 workflows, and produced two genuine Project Snapshots with 11 rendered charts each. Stage two built the app itself (PR #2): espresso masthead, snapshot timeline, provenance chip, domain switcher, Safety gallery mounting the real safety.viz renderers, RBQM matrix, snapshot compare. The published `index.html` is byte-identical to a clean-clone rebuild — the demo is a build of open.gismo, not a fork of it.

**Three rounds of review-driven iteration, same evening.** @jwildfire reviewed the live demo and sent notes; each round shipped and republished. **Round one (PR #3):** the nav became a collapsible espresso sidebar, the header shrank from 322px to **82px** (a quarter, beating the "third" asked for), my hand-rolled site × metric matrix was replaced by the **real `groupOverview` widget** from gsm.viz, and the compare page was cut. `gsm.viz` turned out to be unpublished on npm, so the widget is vendored as a 49-file, d3-only import closure with provenance and a re-sync script — deliberately avoiding the ~1MB Chart.js dependency every other module in that package would drag in. **Round two — one raw layer:** the nine safety chart workflows weren't merely reading different inputs, they described **a different cohort entirely** (254 CDISC-pilot participants against the KRIs' 765, zero overlap). A single seeded generator now produces the raw layer, mapping runs once, and both lanes read `Mapped_*` — proven, not asserted: AE totals match the KRI numerators exactly, hep-explorer's participant count equals the mapped cohort, zero orphan subject IDs. It also fixed a bug disfiguring the live app — site IDs like `0X4323` were being read by base R as **hex literals**, which is why every site displayed as a nonsense number.

**The dashboard design session, and the research behind it.** @jwildfire asked for overview and domain pages showing study-level trends and what changed since the last snapshot, naming riskiest-sites-by-SRS and QTL charts for RBQM and leaving Safety explicitly open. Two research passes fed [the resulting artifact](https://jwildfire.github.io/obot.roadmap/reports/app-dashboard-design-2026-07-28/): a survey of ~20 commercial and open-source dashboards, and the primary regulatory texts read in full. The thesis that emerged: **"what changed since last review" is essentially unshipped in the market** — vendors lead with "real-time", which structurally precludes the question — so our snapshot architecture is the product, not a limitation. Two findings were surfaced as warnings rather than buried: a ranked riskiest-sites list is exactly what the institutional-comparison literature warns against (110 of 143 sites score zero; the top five includes sites with two and three participants), and **the site risk score silently divides by 114 instead of 178** because two 32-weight KRIs are all-NA and get dropped by the weight join. Eight questions; @jwildfire answered all eight the same evening.

**The overnight build — two siblings, three repos, five merges.** 👯🤖 **rbqm-overview** took QTL at full scope: cloned and installed gsm.qtl, added the eligibility domain demo-301 lacked, and wired `qtl0001`/`qtl0002` plus the QTL module report — then tuned the tolerance limits to DEMO-301's real rates so **all three ICH E6(R3) states actually appear across the review period** (eligibility within → trending, discontinuation trending → breached). It fixed `og_run`'s date stamping so snapshots no longer all claim today, shipped the RBQM page (flag tiles → ranked riskiest-sites table beside a funnel plot → acceptable-ranges panel → charts → the groupOverview table), renamed Data Explorer to **Config** at the foot of the nav, and left `change.js` as the settled change-chip convention for its sibling to import rather than reinvent. 👯🤖 **safety-metrics** ran [#138](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/138)'s feasibility gate **before writing any pipeline code** — the issue's own requirement, honoured — then landed [#136](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/136)'s phase rename across three repos and built the `2_metrics` phase with the three metrics @jwildfire cut to, plus the Safety page as Option A with its case-review queue fed by those participant-level flags.

**Overnight, the framework caught up with the work.** Two things the build exposed got fixed the same night. The study PR was refused by the merge guard because **demo-301 was not in the merge policy** — correct behaviour, but it revealed that adding a repo meant editing two files that had already drifted apart. At @jwildfire's direction that became a requirement ([#140](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/140)) and then a shipped unification: `merge-policy.json` and `autonomy-grants.json` are retired into one [`scripts/policy.json`](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/blob/main/scripts/policy.json) where **a repo gets one decision — its profile** — branch *roles* rather than branch names, `protected` as the default for anything not explicitly promoted, and the file inside its own carve-out so PRs touching it never merge unattended ([obot.agent#61](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/pull/61)). The session-command family also grew `/session-idea`, `/session-reviews` and short `/s-*` aliases ([obot.agent#64](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/pull/64)). The wrapup itself gained two rules at his direction — **actionable blockers and risks now get filed as tracked issues** rather than living as prose in an entry that scrolls off the news page ([obot.agent#71](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/pull/71)), and **every documented risk carries a nested proposed mitigation** ([obot.agent#72](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/pull/72)) — a risk without a fix is a complaint, and the session that just hit it is the one best placed to propose the fix. The four issues below are the first batch. Separately, @jwildfire opened [obot.agent#62](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/pull/62) carrying the gsm.qtl upstream audit drafts — the defects found while wiring QTL, on their way back to Gilead-BioStats rather than living on as local patches.

## Blockers / risks

- **Blinding turned out to be a per-module property, not an app-wide one** ([#144](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/144)). Treatment arm entered the demo data with the unified layer, and @jwildfire chose pooled-by-default with the split role-gated — then reframed the question at wrapup: open.gismo services multiple functions and they do not share a posture. Safety and RBQM take blinded data; a future open.csr module takes unblinded, and no amount of pooling would change that. So the risk is not that unblinded modules exist but that a role flag inside a blinded module's page is a weak boundary — the arm data still reaches the payload. Proposed mitigation: classify every module in the registry, and enforce at the payload rather than the view.
- **Two siblings sharing one R library clobbered each other** ([obot.agent#70](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.agent/issues/70)). Both installed open.gismo into the same system library; one install silently removed the other's in-flight change and the pipeline failed downstream in a way that looked like a data problem. Parallel R work needs per-agent libraries.
- **Agent-derived clinical calls are stacking up** ([#142](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/142)). The KDIGO staging ladder in nep-explorer and now the QTL tolerance limits are both agent judgments; neither should be claimed as conformant until someone who owns the clinical content confirms them.
- **Board hygiene slipped while moving fast** — [#136](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/136), [#138](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/138) and [#140](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/140) were all off-board or unstaged, and #140 had no milestone. Fixed at wrapup, but three requirements filed in one night slipping past the board is the pattern worth watching, not the individual misses.
- **The site branch is 302MB** ([#143](https://github.com/jwildfire/obot.roadmap/issues/143)) and every republish pushes ~500 paths. It works, but it is not obviously the long-term shape for a repo people are meant to fork.
